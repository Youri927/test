Eleven steps. Six to twelve weeks.

That is a typical new pool with us, from the first drawing to the handover. You get your own schedule and plan when you sign.

01–02of 11

  1. Steps 1 and 2: Design & planning, engineering & permits

    We learn your goals, the way you live and your budget, assess the property and draw a pool that fits. Engineered plans go in for approval; we handle the permits, local codes, zoning and safety requirements before work starts.

  2. Step 3: Layout & excavation

    The design is staked out on the ground, then dug to specification for the structural work.

  3. Steps 4 and 5: Steel, plumbing & electrical

    A rebar framework runs through the whole structure, so the shell can handle ground movement. Then lines, drains, returns and electrical for filtration, lighting, and any spa or water feature.

  4. Step 6: Shotcrete or gunite shell

    Shotcrete or gunite is sprayed over the steel to form the shell, the foundation of the pool.

  5. Steps 7 and 8: Tile, coping, features & decking

    Waterline tile, coping and the features you chose: spa, sun shelf, waterfalls. Then pavers, travertine or concrete around the pool, laid for durability, safety and looks.

  6. Step 9: Interior finish

    Plaster, quartz or pebble: a smooth, watertight finish that sets the color and texture of the water.

  7. Steps 10 and 11: Fill, startup & handover

    The pool is filled, the equipment started, the water balanced and every system checked. Final inspections for code and quality, then a walkthrough of how to run and care for your pool.

Book leak detection

How we find it

Hydrophone listening, dye testing, visual inspection and targeted tests, chosen for your symptoms and the way your pool is set up.

Where pools leak

The shell, skimmers, return lines, the main drain, light niches, the tile line, spa walls, plumbing, fittings, water features, the equipment pad.

Leak or evaporation?

Set a bucket on the pool step and fill it to the same level as the pool. If the pool drops more than the bucket, there may be a leak. Florida pools do lose water to evaporation, more in hot, sunny, windy or dry weather.

Leak detection questions

Still not sure? Call or text (941) 779-7007.

How do I know if my pool has a leak?

Water dropping faster than normal, wet ground around the pool or the equipment, bubbles from the returns, trouble keeping the pool full, cracks, loose tile, or refilling all the time.

Could it just be evaporation?

Yes. Florida pools lose water to evaporation, more so in hot, sunny, windy or dry weather, and screen cages, waterfalls, spas and water features change how much. If the loss is excessive or happens every day, leak detection is worth it.

How much water loss is normal?

Some loss is normal. If your pool loses more than you would expect, or needs topping off often, have it checked.

What is a bucket test?

Set a bucket on the pool step and fill it to the same level as the pool. If the pool drops more than the bucket, there may be a leak.

What areas of the pool can leak?

The shell, skimmer, return lines, main drain, light niche, tile line, spa wall, plumbing, fittings, water features and the equipment pad.

Do you check both the pool and the spa?

Yes: pool-only systems, pool and spa combinations, and water features. A spa can leak on its own, at the spillway, jets, fittings, drain or plumbing.

What leak detection methods do you use?

Hydrophone listening, dye testing, visual inspection, and targeted tests chosen for your symptoms and the way your pool is set up.

Does leak detection include repairs?

Detection finds the leak. Small epoxy repairs are included; plumbing, structural, tile and larger repairs are quoted separately.

Can you repair the leak once you find it?

Often, yes. We are a licensed pool contractor, so we quote the repair as soon as we find the source. Some leaks call for plumbing, surface, tile or equipment work, or a renovation.

Should I schedule leak detection before resurfacing?

Yes. Resurfacing over a leak that has not been fixed can lead to more damage, more water loss and added cost.

Can a pool leak damage my yard or deck?

Yes. Ongoing water loss can wash out soil, create soft spots, damage decking or pavers, cause erosion and affect the pool structure.

What should I tell you before the appointment?

How much water the pool loses, how often you add water, whether the loss changes with the pump on or off, and any wet, cracked, loose or suspicious areas.

Does the pool need to be full?

Yes, at its normal operating level, so we can check the system and the suspected areas.

Do you offer leak detection near me?

Yes: in Manatee County, Sarasota County and the surrounding communities, along with resurfacing, remodels and new builds.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes. Pool contractor license CPC1461058 and gas license LI45304.
