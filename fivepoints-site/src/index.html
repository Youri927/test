Three kinds of roofs. Each one leaks in its own places.

A pitched shingle roof, the low roof of a mobile home and the flat roof of a commercial building don’t fail the same way. We know where to look first on each one.

The numbers on the drawing match the list.

Where we look first on a house

  1. Pipe flashings, around the vents that come through the roof.
  2. Wall and chimney flashing.
  3. Skylights.
  4. Valleys, where two slopes of the roof meet.

Most leaks on houses come from these spots, when they weren’t installed right. We inspect them on every visit.

Where we look first on a mobile home

  1. Around the pipes that come through the roof.

A small leak on a mobile home is rarely small for long. We work quickly, so you’re not left uncovered.

If your roof is leaking right now

  • Cover what you can’t move with plastic.
  • Catch the water in buckets; a towel at the bottom stops the splashing.
  • Dry everything that got wet: furniture, carpet, woodwork.
  • Call your insurance agent to start the claim.
  • Hire a licensed, insured contractor, and ask about guarantees and permits.

Repair, reroof or replace?

Three answers to a roof that leaks. After the inspection, we go through them with you.

Repair

When the roof is fairly young and the damage stays in one area.

Cracked flashing, broken or missing shingles, vent pipes, skylight leaks, ceiling stains.

Reroof

One new layer of shingles over the old one, with no tear-off: faster, and less expensive.

Only over a single layer, on a roof that is sound underneath. Not with two layers already, soft decking, or for part of a roof.

Replace

When asphalt shingles are past 20 to 25 years, the damage is widespread, or repairs keep coming back.

Bald spots, curled or cupped shingles, dark streaks, moss.

What a full replacement includes

  1. We tear the old roof off, down to the wood deck.

  2. We inspect the deck, and repair or replace what has gone soft.

  3. New underlayment: the waterproof layer on the deck.

  4. New rust-resistant metal flashing where leaks start.

  5. New metal drip edge along the edges of the roof.

Questions we get often.

What is the best roof for a mobile home in Florida?

It depends on your home and your budget. TPO and PVC membranes both handle Florida heat, humidity and UV, and resist mold and mildew; TPO costs less. Metal is light, fire resistant and energy efficient, needs little maintenance and can last up to 50 years.

How often should a mobile home roof be sealed or coated?

It depends on the material. A metal roof every 3 to 5 years; TPO and PVC every couple of years, so they don’t get brittle and crack; flat roofs every two years or after a storm. Florida weather can call for more.

Should I repair my roof or replace it?

It comes down to the age of the roof, how far the damage goes and its overall condition. A young roof with damage in one area is worth repairing. If asphalt shingles are past 20 to 25 years, or you keep paying for repairs, a replacement usually costs less in the long run.

Do I need a roof inspection after a storm?

Yes. High winds, heavy rain or hail can damage a roof without it showing from the ground. An inspection finds the damage, tells you what needs repair, and keeps it from getting worse. Ours is free.

Can you help with my insurance claim?

Yes. We document the damage, write a detailed estimate, and help you through the claim from start to finish.

Can a new roof go over my old one?

Sometimes: that’s reroofing. It works over a single layer of shingles on a roof that is sound underneath. With two layers already, soft decking, or for only part of a roof, we tear off and replace.
